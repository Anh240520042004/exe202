# Agile & Scrum Complete Guide

## Introduction to Agile

Agile is an iterative approach to project management and software development that helps teams deliver value to their customers faster and with fewer headaches.

### The Agile Manifesto

**Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
**Working software** over comprehensive documentation
**Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
**Responding to change** over following a plan

### 12 Principles of Agile

1. Customer satisfaction by early and continuous delivery of valuable software
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development
3. Deliver working software frequently
4. Business people and developers must work together daily
5. Build projects around motivated individuals
6. Face-to-face conversation is the most effective method of communication
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress
8. Sustainable development pace
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence
10. Simplicity - maximize work not done
11. Self-organizing teams
12. Regular reflection and adjustment

## Scrum Framework

Scrum is the most popular Agile framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products.

### The Scrum Team

**Product Owner**
- Maximizes the value of the product
- Manages the Product Backlog
- Makes decisions about features and priorities

**Scrum Master**
- Facilitates Scrum events
- Removes impediments
- Coaches the team

**Development Team**
- Self-organizing and cross-functional
- Delivers increments of working software
- Estimates and commits to work

### Scrum Events

**Sprint**
- Time-boxed iteration (1-4 weeks)
- Consistent duration for rhythm
- No changes that endanger the Sprint Goal

**Sprint Planning**
- What can be delivered in the Sprint?
- How will the work be accomplished?
- Time-box: 8 hours for 1-month Sprint

**Daily Scrum**
- 15 minutes daily
- What did I do yesterday?
- What will I do today?
- Any impediments?

**Sprint Review**
- Inspect the increment
- Gather feedback
- Adapt the Product Backlog

**Sprint Retrospective**
- Inspect the Sprint
- Create improvement plan
- Implement changes in next Sprint

### Scrum Artifacts

**Product Backlog**
- Ordered list of everything in the product
- Dynamic and evolving
- Owned by the Product Owner

**Sprint Backlog**
- Product Backlog items selected for Sprint
- Plus the plan for delivering them
- Owned by the Development Team

**Increment**
- Sum of all completed Product Backlog items
- Must be in usable condition
- "Done" criteria must be met

## User Stories

### Format
"As a [type of user], I want [some goal] so that [some reason]."

### Examples
- "As a student, I want to search for documents so that I can find study materials quickly."
- "As an admin, I want to manage users so that the platform stays organized."

### Story Points

| Size | Points | Description |
|------|--------|-------------|
| XS   | 1      | Very small task |
| S    | 2      | Small task |
| M    | 3      | Medium task |
| L    | 5      | Large task |
| XL   | 8      | Very large task |
| XXL  | 13     | Too large, split it |

---

## Agile Metrics

**Velocity**
- Story points completed per Sprint
- Used for forecasting

**Burndown Chart**
- Shows remaining work over time
- Helps track progress

**Burnup Chart**
- Shows completed work vs total scope
- Tracks scope changes

---

## Best Practices

1. Keep Sprints short (1-2 weeks ideal)
2. Hold Daily Standups at the same time
3. Limit Work in Progress (WIP)
4. Focus on continuous improvement
5. Celebrate successes
6. Address impediments immediately
7. Maintain stable Sprint duration

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Adding work to Sprint mid-way
- Skipping Daily Standups
- Ignoring the Definition of Done
- Not involving the Product Owner
- Underestimating complexity
- Ignoring technical debt
